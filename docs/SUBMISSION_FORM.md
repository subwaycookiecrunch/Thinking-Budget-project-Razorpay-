## What it solves

The Thinking Budget helps developers review a batch of code within a chosen AI review budget. It allocates review effort across files, asks a model to inspect the selected source, validates quoted evidence against that source, and shows findings alongside everything still needing human review. A budget limit or provider failure produces an explicit incomplete result. The developer can inspect and export the decisions, usage, and failures. The demo includes a synthetic payment-idempotency regression, custom code input, and an offline rule-based mode. A reproducible synthetic benchmark measures the allocation trade-off; it is separate from real model results and makes no claim of production accuracy or money saved.

## What broke, and how you got out

The most serious failure was in the evidence: the first version's headline “trained model” improvement came from heuristic traces, and different documents reported different transfer scores. The apparent truncation ablation kept decisions fixed, so unchanged accuracy was built into the experiment. That could have survived a polished demo and failed the first serious review.

The recovery was to audit the generators, withdraw unsupported claims, and make provenance part of the product. The submission now distinguishes live model output, offline rules, and synthetic benchmark results. The review workflow validates findings against source, records the chosen budget and actual usage separately, and leaves failed or unreviewed files in a human-review queue. The synthetic prioritization evaluation reports positive-file coverage, missed positives, safe files reviewed, and source characters read; it does not measure defect detection. A forced provider failure is part of the demo. A subsequently supplied real adapter and training logs are preserved, with their weak tool-use results disclosed rather than treated as proof of improvement.

## Short fallback for a tight text limit

The original headline model gains were generated by heuristic traces; inconsistent scores and a non-causal ablation exposed the problem. I audited the generators, withdrew those claims, and separated live model runs from offline rules and synthetic benchmarks. The product now exposes source evidence, usage, unreviewed files, and failure events. Budget exhaustion or provider failure leaves a human-review task instead of implying completion. The demo deliberately includes that failure.
